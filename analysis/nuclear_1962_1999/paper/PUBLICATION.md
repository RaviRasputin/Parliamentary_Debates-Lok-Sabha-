# Path to publication (updated 26 October 2026)

## Where the paper stands

- [x] **Validated measure.** Two blind Claude coders with Opus adjudication.
  - 1962–99: 2,954 passages (alpha 0.94 relevance, 0.88 stance).
  - 2000–08: 1,493 passages (alpha 0.97, 0.91).
- [x] **Lok Sabha party data**, 1962–2004 (54% of member turns matched).
- [x] **Robustness.** The broad and narrow scopes, each house separately, and the series without
  the security frame all point the same way.
- [x] **Extension to 2008** (the Indo-US deal, H6).
- [x] **Replication files** in this folder (see `CLAUDE.md`, `PROMPTS.md`, `validation/`).
- [ ] **Human validation of stance.** The author's codes agree with the model's in only 24% of
  cases (alpha 0.11, n = 21). Before submission, train a second human coder with the codebook
  and worked examples, have them code R001–R030, and report human–human and human–model
  agreement. Reviewers will ask for this first.
- [ ] **Rajya Sabha party labels, by-elections, the 1969 Congress split.** These need
  `sansad.in`, `rajyasabha.nic.in` or TCPD data.
- [ ] **Archival triangulation.** Wilson Center NPIHP, *FRUS* South Asia volumes, Abhilekh Patal.
  These need the domains opened.
- [ ] **A placebo topic.** Run the same measure on a non-nuclear foreign-policy topic, to show the
  convergence is specific to nuclear policy.
- [ ] **Theory.** Engage Putnam's two-level games and the literature on parliaments and foreign
  policy (for example Raunio and Wagner on parliamentary involvement in foreign and security
  policy; verify the citations).
- [ ] **Check the references.** They were compiled from memory: check editions and pages.
- [ ] **Deposit** the code and coded data on Zenodo or Harvard Dataverse with a DOI.
- [ ] **Feedback.** Present at ISA, BISA or the Annual Conference on South Asia (Madison), and
  send the draft to two or three specialists in Indian nuclear history.

## Journals

| Journal | Publisher | Why it fits | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *India Review* | Taylor & Francis | Indian politics and foreign policy; publishes nuclear-history and data-driven work | Best first target |
| *The Nonproliferation Review* | Taylor & Francis (James Martin CNS) | Nuclear decision-making and proliferation history; values new primary sources | Expects strong engagement with the nonproliferation literature |
| *Asian Survey* | University of California Press | Asian politics; shorter, accessible articles | Tight length; little room for method |
| *Contemporary South Asia* | Taylor & Francis | Interdisciplinary South Asia; open to political history | Broad audience; frame the nuclear stakes clearly |
| *Journal of Strategic Studies* | Taylor & Francis | Strategy and nuclear history | High bar; needs the archival triangulation |
| *Journal of Legislative Studies* | Taylor & Francis | Legislatures and their influence; comparative audience | Must speak to the comparative legislative literature |
| *Studies in Indian Politics* | SAGE (Lokniti–CSDS) | Indian politics; open to quantitative text analysis | Smaller international readership |

Stretch targets once the archival work is done: *International Security* and *Foreign Policy
Analysis*.

Suggested order: *India Review* or *The Nonproliferation Review* first. Choose the *Journal of
Legislative Studies* if the paper is framed around parliaments in general.

Consider a second, short data paper describing the corpora and the coded samples, which others
can cite.

Check each journal's current author guidelines (scope, word limit, citation style, open-access
fees, and its policy on LLM-assisted coding and disclosure) before submitting.
